# Audience Analysis

---

The portfolio has multiple audiences.

Each arrives with different motivations.

Each spends different amounts of time.

Each values different information.

The experience should support all of them without feeling fragmented.

---

# Audience 1

## Startup Founder

### Goal

Find someone capable of building products.

Not merely writing code.

### Time

2–5 minutes.

### Questions

Can this person build independently?

Can they think beyond implementation?

Can they communicate clearly?

Do they understand users?

Can they ship?

### What attracts them

Clear thinking.

Systems.

Product ownership.

Evidence.

### What they ignore

Technology buzzwords.

Long biographies.

Skill percentages.

---

# Audience 2

## Engineering Manager

### Goal

Understand technical depth.

### Time

5–10 minutes.

### Questions

Can this person engineer scalable systems?

Do they understand architecture?

How do they solve problems?

How do they communicate technical decisions?

### What attracts them

Architecture.

Trade-offs.

Interesting engineering.

Performance.

Backend thinking.

### What they ignore

Fancy animations without purpose.

---

# Audience 3

## Recruiter

### Goal

Determine fit quickly.

### Time

30–90 seconds.

### Questions

Who is this?

What role?

What experience?

Where are the projects?

How do I contact them?

### What attracts them

Clarity.

Readable typography.

Simple navigation.

Professional presentation.

### What they ignore

Complex interactions.

Deep architecture.

---

# Audience 4

## Product Manager

### Goal

Understand product thinking.

### Questions

Does this person understand users?

Can they define problems?

Can they prioritize?

Can they simplify?

### Important

Product managers care less about code.

They care more about decisions.

---

# Audience 5

## Designers

### Goal

Evaluate interaction quality.

### Questions

Is the motion meaningful?

Is the hierarchy intentional?

Is typography well considered?

Is the experience memorable?

### What attracts them

Consistency.

Pacing.

Interaction quality.

Whitespace.

Micro interactions.

---

# Audience 6

## Frontend Engineers

### Goal

Understand implementation quality.

### Questions

How was this built?

What libraries?

Performance?

Architecture?

Animations?

Three.js?

Accessibility?

### Important

Do not expose implementation immediately.

Reward curiosity.

# Design Implications

Recruiters should understand the portfolio within 30 seconds.

Founders should enjoy exploring for 5 minutes.

Engineers should still discover new details after 10 minutes.

The experience should become deeper with curiosity.

Never overwhelm casual visitors.

Never bore technical visitors.

---

# Information Layers

Every project should reveal information progressively.

Layer 1

Visual identity.

One sentence.

---

Layer 2

Problem.

Solution.

Outcome.

---

Layer 3

Architecture.

Stack.

Engineering decisions.

---

Layer 4

Implementation details.

Repository.

Technical documentation.

Only users who intentionally explore should reach this level.

---

# Final Principle

Different audiences should feel that the portfolio was designed specifically for them,

even though they all experience the same website.

That should be achieved through progressive disclosure,

not separate versions of the portfolio.
